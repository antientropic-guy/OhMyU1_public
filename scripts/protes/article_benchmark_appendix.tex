\section{PROTES comparison: exact indicators and data-based initialization}
\label{app:protes_protocol}

\subsection{Exact assignment indicator, ranks, and storage}
Let $x_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$ satisfy unit row and column sums. We order the $N=n^2$
bits row by row. After $k=rn+s$ bits, $0\le s<n$, a state records the set
$S$ of occupied columns and $h=|S|-r\in\{0,1\}$, indicating whether the
partially processed row already contains its unit entry. A state is retained
only if it is reachable from the empty prefix and admits a feasible suffix.
For $h=0$, at least one unoccupied column must remain among columns
$s+1,\ldots,n$; for $h=1$, at least one occupied column must lie among
columns $1,\ldots,s$. A transition assigns the next bit, preserves the
one-per-row rule and column uniqueness, and updates $S$. The endpoints are
$S=\varnothing$ and $S=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. Assigning value one to each allowed
transition gives one unit-amplitude path per permutation, hence the complete
indicator without enumerating all $n!$ solutions.

With out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero, the number of
states at an internal cut is
\begin{equation}
 R_k=\binom nr-\binom{s}{r-n+s}
       +\binom n{r+1}-\binom{n-s}{r+1},\qquad R_0=R_N=1.
\end{equation}
The first pair of terms counts $h=0$ states and the second counts $h=1$
states. After grouping prefixes and suffixes by residual constraint charge,
the indicator unfolding consists of disjoint nonempty all-ones blocks,
each of rank one. Consequently $R_k$ is the exact minimal ordinary TT rank
for this ordering. The maximum is $\Theta(2^n/\sqrt n)$ (e.g., 125 at $n=8$,
461 at $n=10$, and 24309 at $n=16$). The $U(1)$ representation has the same
total link dimension, but every sector has degeneracy one. This exact-rank
statement concerns the full indicator; an optimizer's distribution concentrated
on a smaller feasible subset need not have these ranks.

The two dense float64 storage counts in \cref{fig:protes_exact} are
\begin{equation}
 B_{\rm uniform}=8[2(N-2)R_{\max}^2+4R_{\max}],\qquad
 B_{\rm variable}=16\sum_{k=1}^{N}R_{k-1}R_k.
\end{equation}
They describe the fast PROTES core layout and the variable-rank
\texttt{protes\_general} layout, respectively. For $U(1)$, the number of
allowed scalar transitions at position $(r,s)$ is
\begin{equation}
 E_{r,s}=\binom{n-1}{r}+\binom nr-\binom{s+1}{r-n+s+1}
                 +\binom n{r+1}-\binom{n-s}{r+1}.
\end{equation}
The first term counts transitions assigning one and the remaining terms
count transitions assigning zero. Thus numerical block storage is
$8\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}E_{r,s}$ bytes. These counts were checked
against the constructed blocks and dense arrays. MiB and GiB denote $2^{20}$
and $2^{30}$ bytes. Full $U(1)$ objects were constructed and measured through
$n=16$ using \texttt{Base.summarysize}, including dictionaries, charge vectors,
the constraint matrix, and other reachable fields. The measurement excludes
process/runtime memory and temporary buffers; it depends on object sharing
in this constructor. Dense curves count coefficient arrays only. Adam states,
gradients, and training workspaces are excluded from all storage claims.

Both upstream PROTES interfaces in the checked revision use dense arrays;
passing JAX \texttt{BCOO} cores fails at an unsupported summation, whereas
dense cores, including variable ranks, pass the same sampling/training check.
This establishes the absence of a working drop-in sparse path in this
revision, not the impossibility of implementing sparse PROTES. ``Dense''
refers to TT cores, not an explicitly stored tensor of $2^N$ entries.

\subsection{Untrained full-indicator sampling protocol}
Both models sample uniformly over the same full feasible set. PROTES uses
the above zero/one transition cores, padded to a common internal rank for
its fast implementation and left at their exact ranks for
\texttt{protes\_general}. For OhMyU1, backward dynamic programming computes
the number $C_k(q)$ of feasible continuations from each residual charge.
The scalar on an allowed edge $q\to q'$ is replaced by
$\sqrt{C_{k+1}(q')/C_k(q)}$. Products telescope to $1/\sqrt{n!}$, giving an
exact right-canonical normalized amplitude. Its squared amplitudes and the
PROTES probability indicator therefore induce the same uniform law.
The number of complete paths and normalization are checked explicitly.

We call the unchanged OhMyU1 serial \texttt{sample\_nondeg!} and the upstream
PROTES sampling kernels with their usual JAX vectorization. There is no
objective function, training dataset, learning rate, or optimization iteration
in this experiment. Batches contain 100 samples. Each of three seeds runs
until cumulative timed generator calls reach at least one second; a final
batch can exceed that duration. Throughput divides the actual generated
count by actual timed duration, and counts repetitions. Construction,
canonicalization, JIT warm-up, feasibility checks, and unique-point bookkeeping
are excluded. PROTES timing includes materializing the output and recomputing
right environments per batch, as in its normal sampling loop.
The feasible fraction is 100.0\% in all recorded batches. Raw per-trial
batch counts, times, warm-up/construction times, and distinct-point counts
are retained in the accompanying results snapshot. Full dense models are
allocated for $n=4,\ldots,10$; beyond this grid their storage curves are
analytical, not timing measurements. OhMyU1 is measured for $n=4,\ldots,16$.

The large $U(1)$ object traversal initially caused memory pressure at
$n=15,16$ on the local machine. Final sampling measurements were repeated
without repeating that deep traversal and with garbage collection before
warm-up; the initial memory-audit runs are retained separately. The shaded
ranges report run-to-run variation, not confidence intervals or an
asymptotic complexity result.

\subsection{Paired, data-initialized optimization protocol}
These diagnostic instances are separate from the article's held-out objective
files. For each $(n,j,o)$, with repetition index $j$ and objective family $o$,
NumPy's generator uses seed $20261008+1000n+10j+\mathbf{1}_{o=\mathrm{quadratic}}$.
It first draws 400 independent random permutations and converts them to
row-major binary assignments. It then draws either independent
$c_i\sim U[-50,50]$, with cost $c^Tx$, or a matrix with independent
$B_{ij}\sim U[-7,7]$, sets $Q=(B+B^T)/2$, and uses cost $x^TQx$.
Exactly the same arrays are exported to Julia; SHA-256 hashes and initial
best costs are checked across languages. Identical integer seeds alone
would not ensure identical arrays across their random-number generators.

Define novelty as the number of distinct generated feasible points outside
the set of initial observations. Initial duplicates are not new, repeated
generated points count once, and the initial best cost is retained in every
method. The 5-second screen uses $n=4,\ldots,10$ and $j=0$; the 50-second
comparison uses $n=8$ and $j=0,1,2$, for both objective families. All five
PROTES configurations in \cref{tab:protes_settings} are included at both
budgets. At $n=4$ the particular initial datasets cover all 24 solutions.

PROTES starts from its ordinary random dense cores, receives the initial
observations through its external-sample interface in deterministically
shuffled passes, and applies its normal elite update. Thus only the best
10.0\% of each input batch contributes to the likelihood update, not all
400 observations simultaneously. This explicit initialization adapter is
not claimed to be a published PROTES fitting procedure. After initialization
no more external observations are injected. The external evaluator assigns
valid points score $(2/\pi)\arctan C(x)$ and invalid points score 2, preserving
the ordering of feasible costs. An entirely infeasible batch uses the
upstream skip-update path. If fewer than $k_{\rm top}$ points are feasible,
the unchanged elite rule also includes penalized infeasible points. These
choices can affect learning and are part of the scope of the conclusion.
No mask, repair, indicator, $A,b$, or permutation encoding is given to PROTES.

OhMyU1 receives $A,b$, deduplicates and cost-sorts the same initial data,
and rebuilds its MPS from the retained data at each outer iteration, applying
EELS with $\gamma=1$ and sector degeneracy one before canonicalization.
Training uses one right--left sweep, learning rate 0.05, and normalized
weights proportional to $\exp[-(C(x)-C_{\min})/T]$, with $T$ the sample
standard deviation of the current costs; weights are uniform if $T=0$.
The normalized trained MPS supplies up to 10000 samples, processed in batches
of 100 for deadline checks. The next dataset contains up to 400 lowest-cost
distinct points from the union of retained and newly generated points.
No external feasible generator is called after initialization. These are
the article's EELS updates with the fixed 20-iteration cap replaced by a
wall-time limit; realized iteration counts are reported below.

Initial model construction/fitting, subsequent training, sampling, novelty
bookkeeping, and objective evaluation all count toward the 5/50-second
budgets. Explicit warm-up and file loading do not: PROTES warms its kernels
on a synthetic binary objective at the same dimension, while OhMyU1 warms
one full outer iteration and discards its model. Residual compilation counts
toward the measured budget. Deadlines are cooperative: a current sample or
training operation can overrun, and a batch arriving after the deadline is
not accepted. All initial fits completed. There is no fixed number of
PROTES optimization iterations; generated counts and batch sizes determine
the sampling workload, whereas only batches containing feasible points
trigger further Adam steps. Total post-initialization Adam-update counts
were not separately logged. The result tables give generated counts and
actual times rather than implying equal iteration counts.

All experiments ran locally on an Intel Core i5-10210U (four physical cores,
eight logical processors, approximately 8 GB RAM), Windows 11, with each
process pinned to logical CPU 0 and BLAS restricted to one thread. OhMyU1
uses Julia 1.12.5 with one thread; PROTES uses Python 3.12.14, NumPy 1.26.4,
JAX 0.4.38 (CPU/x64), and Optax 0.2.4. The upstream revision is
\texttt{06d447845dd26ffccbc8e95c7545fa1d2617940e}; its kernels were not edited.
Frequency scaling and operating-system activity were not controlled.
The historical PROTES runs and subsequent OhMyU1 runs were not interleaved,
so wall-time comparisons retain possible temporal system-load effects.
The scripts, compact results, hashes, and Julia plotting code accompany
the notebook \texttt{examples/PROTES\_assignment\_scaling.ipynb}.

These small diagnostic grids establish neither a statistically stable failure
threshold nor an optimized comparison of final costs. In particular, the
50-second \texttt{fit20} setting generates new feasible points for every seed,
despite the zero counts of the default setting. Feasible-set sparsity
$n!/2^{n^2}$ is suggestive but does not describe a learned distribution's
mass on feasible points. More extensive training, alternative initialization,
different penalty/update rules, or supplying a low-rank exact indicator can
change the result. Few or simple constraints may produce small TT ranks and
very different trade-offs. All claims here are restricted to the measured
assignment setting, with no universal superiority claim.
